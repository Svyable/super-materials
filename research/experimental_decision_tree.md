# Experimental decision tree

## Gate A — did the intended phase form?

Require structural/spectroscopic evidence at the synthesis condition. If the target phase is absent, optimize chemistry/pathway rather than interpreting transport.

## Gate B — does a transport transition belong to that phase?

Measure four-probe resistance with field and current-density dependence on a state whose XRD/Raman fingerprint is known. If the phase changes between measurements, create a new `state_id` and do not combine the evidence.

## Gate C — bulk or filamentary?

A phase-linked transport transition promotes the sample to local magnetometry / calibrated susceptibility and, when technically feasible, thermodynamic evidence. Small shielding fraction or spatially localized signal is reported as filamentary/surface until demonstrated otherwise.

## Gate D — mechanism checks

Use matched H/D phases, gap spectroscopy, critical fields and comparison to calculated alpha2F where they are discriminating. An isotope shift is uninterpretable if the isotope samples are different structures or stoichiometries.

## Gate E — replication

Release exact preparation path, raw data, state metadata, competing-phase fingerprints and kill criteria. Independent reproduction of phase + transition + decisive controls is the criterion for the strongest claim language.

## Q-MRH6 first-wave protocol

1. Reproduce Mg2RhH5 precursor and Mg2RhH6 conversion above 30 GPa.
2. Establish same-state structure + transport baseline.
3. Compare fast cryogenic decompression with staged decompression on matched cells.
4. Stop at pre-registered pressure points; log elapsed time and thermal excursions.
5. Re-identify phase and repeat transport/field sweeps at every stop.
6. Run an isotope-matched branch.
7. Escalate to magnetic/gap probes only if lower-cost gates survive.

The result is useful even if negative: it maps the kinetic survival boundary of an experimentally established superconducting hydride.
